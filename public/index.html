The poem in brief

A few weeks in the tenth year of a war

The Iliad is an epic poem traditionally credited to Homer, and one of the oldest works of Western literature. It does not tell the whole Trojan War. It zooms in on a few weeks of the war's tenth year, when Achilles, the greatest Greek warrior, quarrels with his commander Agamemnon and refuses to fight.

Without him the Greeks are driven back to their ships, his friend Patroclus is killed, and Achilles returns in fury to kill Troy's champion, Hector. The poem ends not in victory but in shared grief, as Hector's father Priam ransoms his son's body.
