Who fought for whom

When Zeus lets the gods join the battle in Book 20, Homer lists exactly who stands where. Focus on one side, or tap anyone for their profile.

Zeus, above both sides

He keeps his promise to Thetis and weighs the fates of heroes on golden scales, but he fights for neither army.

The Greeks

Homer calls them Achaeans, Danaans and Argives.

Gods

Heroes

Forces

Contingents from all over Greece, listed in the Catalogue of Ships (Book 2): Mycenae, Sparta, Pylos, Ithaca, Salamis, Crete, the Myrmidons of Phthia and many more.

Troy and its allies

Defending their city with help from across Asia Minor and Thrace.

Gods

Also Leto, and the river god Xanthus (Scamander).

Heroes

Allies

Listed in Book 2: Dardanians under Aeneas, Lycians under Sarpedon and Glaucus, Thracians, Paeonians, Mysians, Phrygians, Carians and others.

Hera & Athena

Book 24 hints at the Judgment of Paris: the Trojan prince insulted them by choosing Aphrodite.

Poseidon

He built Troy's walls for King Laomedon, who refused to pay. He reminds Apollo of it in Book 21.

Aphrodite & Apollo

Aphrodite protects Paris and her son Aeneas. Apollo has a temple on Troy's citadel and shields Hector.
