    After the Iliad

    The Iliad ends with Hector’s funeral, not with the fall of Troy. The rest of the story was told in other poems, now mostly lost, which is where the prologue and the epilogue come from. The Odyssey follows Odysseus on his long voyage home.

    The text

    The chapters retell a condensed version of the story in plain modern English. Every quotation is taken word for word from Homer, The Iliad, translated by Samuel Butler (1898), revised by Timothy Power and Gregory Nagy, from the Perseus Digital Library, Tufts University, used under CC BY-SA 4.0. Omissions are marked with an ellipsis, and each quotation gives its book and line numbers.

    The numbers

    Line counts and the ship count come from the Greek text in the Perseus Digital Library. The day count follows the usual reckoning of the poem’s timeline; the spans it shows are stated in the poem itself.

    The pictures

    All the illustrations are drawn in code, in the manner of Greek vase painting: black figures on clay for scenes by day, clay-red figures on black for scenes by night, and the gods in gold. Sounds are made in the browser.
